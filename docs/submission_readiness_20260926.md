## Publication judgment

The project has the ingredients of a focused methodological paper, but the
novelty is narrower than a new coefficient or a new general inference principle.
A credible publication case is an explicitly delimited inferential study of
an existing class of radius correlations, with a particular fitted robust
reference chain, fully worked regular inference, and a reproducible failure
mechanism. Whether that problem-specific increment merits publication is an
editorial judgment, not a consequence of the amount of code, number of tables,
or number of tests.

The regular theorem uses established Bahadur, Z-estimation, empirical-process
and delta-method tools. Segers also supplies a direct estimated-center
absolute-moment antecedent, now credited in Section 3. Providing their correct specialization is useful,
but by itself is likely an incremental theoretical contribution. The more
distinctive part is the connected analysis: paired reference errors, a direct
location path even at fixed scale, amplification from full fitting, and the
limits of a scalar local-conditioning explanation. Those mechanisms must be
judged against the closest prior work and retained at their actual logical
status (exact identities/model proposition, empirical intervention, heuristic).

The literature search identified a closer antecedent than magnitude correlation
alone: Fout and Fosdick's centered Fréchet correlation already uses correlations
of distances from population and fitted Fréchet means. Thus even the *idea of
using estimated centers before correlating radii* is not new. In one dimension
with Euclidean distance its arithmetic-mean reference version has the same
correlation operation. Replacing the mean by a Huber/MAD reference is a target
choice, not independently a sufficient novelty claim. See the separate overlap
matrix for checked formulas, version status and the narrower inferential gap.

The practical limitations remain consequential. Both nonzero lognormal studies
have active nuisance but strong covariance cancellation and persistent
undercoverage. They support the complete formula and the limits paper; they do
not show a broadly superior studentizer, a calibrated finite-sample workflow,
or a practically validated robust-correlation replacement. An application has
not passed the paired-IID design gate. No favorable benchmark or application
will be manufactured to change that judgment.

A suitable working characterization is **a technically developed author draft
with a plausible, specialized publication contribution**, rather than a
submission-ready article or a major general statistical innovation. The main
editorial risk is an insufficiently motivated specialization of known tools.
The answer is precise positioning and a concise mechanism-led narrative, not
additional distribution grids. The target question and why its inferential
failure matters should remain visible before the technical construction.
