# Appendix: asymptotic theory for robust paired-salience concordance

Date: 2026-08-19

## A.1 Scope and notation

This appendix gives a formal proof draft for the one-dimensional, uncapped,
continuous-margin statistic.  It treats the profile correlation as the
primary estimand and the historical c_delta scale as a smooth corollary.

Let (Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)), (i=1,\ldots,n), be iid from (P).  For a generic
margin (W), define

\[
m=\operatorname{Med}(W),\qquad
d=\operatorname{Med}|W-m|,\qquad s=kd,
\]

where (k>0) is fixed.  Let (T) be the unique solution of

\[
E\left[\psi_c\left(\frac{W-T}{s}\right)\right]=0,
\qquad
\psi_c(u)=\max(-c,\min(u,c)).
\]

For the two margins, put

\[
a=|X-T_X|,\qquad b=|Y-T_Y|,
\]

and define

\[
\mu_a=E(a),\quad \mu_b=E(b),\quad \nu=E(ab),
\quad q_a=E(a^2),\quad q_b=E(b^2).
\]

The two effect scales are

\[
C(P)=\frac{\nu}{\mu_a\mu_b}
\]

and

\[
\rho_P(P)=
\frac{\nu-\mu_a\mu_b}
{\{(q_a-\mu_a^2)(q_b-\mu_b^2)\}^{1/2}}.
\]

Whenever both profile variances are positive,

\[
C=1+\rho_PCV(a)CV(b).
\]

## A.2 Assumptions

For each margin, assume:

**A1 (iid sampling).**  The pairs (Z_i) are iid.  The theorem does not treat
rooms as iid when buildings are the independent sampling units.

**A2 (regular median and MAD).**  The median (m) is unique, (d>0), and the
marginal cdf has a density (f) that is continuous and strictly positive in
neighbourhoods of (m), (m-d), and (m+d).

**A3 (regular Huber root).**  The Huber equation has a unique root (T),

\[
A=P(|U|<c)>0,\qquad U=(W-T)/(kd),
\]

and the distribution has no mass at (T) or (T\pm ckd).

**A4 (profile nondegeneracy).**  The means (mu_a,mu_b) and variances

\[
v_a=q_a-\mu_a^2,\qquad v_b=q_b-\mu_b^2
\]

are strictly positive.  The asymptotic variance of the estimand being tested
is finite and strictly positive.

**A5 (moments).**  For some (eta>0),

\[
E\{|a|^{2+\eta}+|b|^{2+\eta}+|ab|^{2+\eta}\}<\infty.
\]

This is stronger than needed for the basic iid CLT but supplies uniform
integrability for the plug-in and resampling arguments.

**A6 (empirical roots).**  The selected empirical medians, MADs, and Huber
roots are measurable and consistent.  Generalized empirical quantile roots
satisfy their estimating equations up to (o_P(n^{-1/2})).

**A7 (variance plug-in).**  The density estimators used at the median and the
two MAD boundaries are uniformly consistent on fixed neighbourhoods of those
points.  All other empirical moments in the complete influence function are
consistent.

For a pathwise statement, let (mathcal T) consist of signed tangent measures
(h) with total mass zero, finite (2+\eta) weighted variation, and marginal
cdf perturbations continuous at (m) and (m\pm d).  This excludes an atom
inserted exactly at a quantile boundary, where a convention-dependent
one-sided derivative can occur.  The empirical-process tangent generated by
continuous iid sampling is covered.

## A.3 Marginal nuisance derivative

For a marginal tangent (h), write

\[
H(t)=\int 1(w\le t)\,dh(w).
\]

### Lemma A.1 (median and MAD)

Under A2, the median and MAD maps are Hadamard differentiable tangentially to
(mathcal T), with

\[
\dot m[h]=-\frac{H(m)}{f(m)}
\]

and

\[
\dot d[h]=-
\frac{
H(m+d)-H(m-d)+\{f(m+d)-f(m-d)\}\dot m[h]
}{f(m+d)+f(m-d)}.
\]

#### Proof

The median satisfies (F(m)=1/2).  For a differentiable path
(F_t=F+tH+o(t)) and (m_t=m+t\dot m+o(t)), expansion at (m) gives

\[
0=H(m)+f(m)\dot m,
\]

which proves the first display.  The MAD is the positive solution of

\[
F(m+d)-F(m-d)=1/2.
\]

Expanding both boundaries along the same path gives

\[
0=H(m+d)-H(m-d)
+\{f(m+d)-f(m-d)\}\dot m
+\{f(m+d)+f(m-d)\}\dot d.
\]

The denominator is positive by A2, giving the result.  The usual quantile
Hadamard-differentiability theorem makes these implicit expansions uniform on
the declared tangent set.  ∎

For the contamination tangent (h=\Delta_z-P), Lemma A.1 becomes

\[
IF_m(z)=\frac{1/2-1(z\le m)}{f(m)}
\]

and

\[
IF_d(z)=
\frac{
1/2-1(|z-m|\le d)
-\{f(m+d)-f(m-d)\}IF_m(z)
}{f(m+d)+f(m-d)}.
\]

### Lemma A.2 (Huber location with MAD scale)

Under A2–A3, let

\[
B=E\{U1(|U|<c)\}.
\]

Then the Huber location is Hadamard differentiable on the same tangent set and

\[
\dot T[h]
=\frac{s}{A}\int\psi_c\left(\frac{w-T}{s}\right)dh(w)
-\frac{B}{A}\dot s[h],
\qquad \dot s[h]=k\dot d[h].
\]

Consequently,

\[
IF_T(z)=
\frac{s}{A}\psi_c\left(\frac{z-T}{s}\right)
-\frac{B}{A}IF_s(z).
\]

#### Proof

Let

\[
\Psi(P,T,s)=P\psi_c\{(W-T)/s\}.
\]

The absence of mass at the Huber knots permits differentiation under the
integral.  At the population solution,

\[
\partial_T\Psi=-A/s,\qquad \partial_s\Psi=-B/s.
\]

The derivative in the distribution direction is

\[
\partial_P\Psi[h]=\int\psi_c\{(w-T)/s\}\,dh(w).
\]

Since (A>0), the implicit-function theorem applied to
(Psi(P,T(P),s(P))=0) gives the displayed derivative.  Composition with
Lemma A.1 completes the proof.  ∎

### Nuisance Jacobian and local identification

For the unscaled parameter ((m,d,T)), the derivative of the three estimating
equations is

\[
J_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
f(m)&0&0\\
f(m+d)-f(m-d)&f(m+d)+f(m-d)&0\\
0&-B/d&-A/(kd)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Measuring all nuisance perturbations in units of (d) gives

\[
J=J_0\operatorname{diag}(d,d,d)=
\begin{pmatrix}
df(m)&0&0\\
d\{f(m+d)-f(m-d)\}&d\{f(m+d)+f(m-d)\}&0\\
0&-B&-A/k
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

A2–A3 make this triangular matrix nonsingular.  These assumptions guarantee
pointwise regularity; they do not impose a uniform lower bound on its smallest
singular value.

## A.4 Joint moment derivatives

Define

\[
g_X=E\{\operatorname{sign}(X-T_X)\},\quad
h_X=E\{\operatorname{sign}(X-T_X)b\},
\]

with analogous (g_Y,h_Y).  For a joint tangent (h), direct differentiation
and Lemma A.2 give

\[
\dot\mu_a[h]=\int a\,dh-g_X\dot T_X[h],
\]

\[
\dot\mu_b[h]=\int b\,dh-g_Y\dot T_Y[h],
\]

and

\[
\dot\nu[h]=\int ab\,dh-h_X\dot T_X[h]-h_Y\dot T_Y[h].
\]

The second moments satisfy

\[
\dot q_a[h]=\int a^2\,dh-2E(X-T_X)\dot T_X[h],
\]

and analogously for (q_b).  A5 supplies an integrable envelope; the absence
of mass at (T_X,T_Y) removes the absolute-value derivative ambiguity.

For point contamination (z=(x,y)), these derivatives are the corresponding
influence functions.  In particular,

\[
IF_C(z)=
\frac{IF_\nu(z)}{\mu_a\mu_b}
-C\frac{IF_{\mu_a}(z)}{\mu_a}
-C\frac{IF_{\mu_b}(z)}{\mu_b}.
\]

This is one **paired** influence value.  Its variance includes all X–Y,
numerator–denominator, direct–nuisance, and cross-nuisance covariance terms.

## A.5 Profile-correlation derivative

Let

\[
u=\nu-\mu_a\mu_b,\quad
v_a=q_a-\mu_a^2,\quad v_b=q_b-\mu_b^2,
\quad D=(v_av_b)^{1/2}.
\]

For moment order ((\nu,\mu_a,\mu_b,q_a,q_b)), ordinary differentiation gives

\[
\nabla\rho_P=
\left(
\frac1D,
-\frac{\mu_b}{D}+\rho_P\frac{\mu_a}{v_a},
-\frac{\mu_a}{D}+\rho_P\frac{\mu_b}{v_b},
-\frac{\rho_P}{2v_a},
-\frac{\rho_P}{2v_b}
\right).
\]

Combining this gradient with the five complete moment influences defines

\[
IF_{\rho}(z)=\nabla\rho_P^\top IF_M(z),
\]

where (IF_M) denotes the vector of the complete influences of
((\nu,\mu_a,\mu_b,q_a,q_b)), including both Huber/MAD location pathways.

## A.6 Main asymptotic theorem

### Theorem A.1 (asymptotic linearity and normality)

Under A1–A6,

\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\rho_P-\rho_P\}
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n IF_\rho(Z_i;P)+o_P(1),
\]

and

\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat C-C\}
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n IF_C(Z_i;P)+o_P(1).
\]

Therefore

\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\rho_P-\rho_P\}\Rightarrow N(0,V_\rho),
\qquad
\sqrt n\{\widehat C-C\}\Rightarrow N(0,V_C),
\]

where (V_\rho=E(IF_\rho^2)) and (V_C=E(IF_C^2)).

#### Proof

Lemmas A.1–A.2 establish pathwise differentiability and asymptotic linearity
of the six marginal nuisance functionals.  Equivalently, the standard
Z-estimator expansion of their stacked estimating equations gives

\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta)
=-J_0^{-1}\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n g(W_i;\theta)+o_P(1).
\]

The discontinuous indicator equations for median and MAD are covered by the
positive-density quantile expansion; the Huber equation is locally Lipschitz
and has nonsingular derivative.  Consistency in A6 localizes all empirical
roots to the neighbourhoods where these expansions apply.

The maps from the empirical distribution and nuisance locations to the five
moments are Hadamard differentiable by the calculation in Section A.4.
Under A5 their local function classes have square-integrable envelopes and are
stochastically equicontinuous.  Substituting the nuisance expansions therefore
produces the five-dimensional asymptotic linear representation with complete
moment influence (IF_M).

Under A4, the maps

\[
(\nu,\mu_a,\mu_b)\mapsto \nu/(\mu_a\mu_b)
\]

and

\[
(\nu,\mu_a,\mu_b,q_a,q_b)
\mapsto
\frac{\nu-\mu_a\mu_b}
{\{(q_a-\mu_a^2)(q_b-\mu_b^2)\}^{1/2}}
\]

are continuously differentiable in a neighbourhood of the population
moments.  The multivariate delta method yields the two displayed asymptotic
linear representations.  A5 places both influence functions in (L_2(P)),
so the iid central limit theorem gives the asserted normal limits.  ∎

### Corollary A.1 (studentized Wald inference)

Under A1–A7, if

\[
\widehat V_\rho=\frac1{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
(\widehat{IF}_{\rho,i}-\overline{\widehat{IF}_\rho})^2,
\]

and similarly for (C), then

\[
\widehat V_\rho\to_P V_\rho,\qquad \widehat V_C\to_P V_C.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\frac{\sqrt n(\widehat\rho_P-\rho_P)}{\sqrt{\widehat V_\rho}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1),
\]

with the analogous conclusion for (C).

#### Proof

A7 and consistency of the nuisance roots give pointwise convergence of every
plug-in influence component.  A5 supplies uniform integrability of their
squares, hence (L_2) convergence of the empirical influence values and
consistency of the empirical second moment.  Apply Slutsky's theorem to
Theorem A.1.  ∎

## A.7 Permutation statements

### Proposition A.1 (fixed-margin equivalence)

For any observed nonconstant positive profile vectors (a,b), and any
permutation (pi),

\[
C_\pi=1+\rho_\pi CV_n(a)CV_n(b).
\]

The two CV factors are positive and unchanged over the permutation orbit.
Thus (C_\pi) and (ho_\pi) have identical ranks, one-sided permutation
p-values, and two-sided p-values when each is centred at its permutation-null
value (1) or (0), respectively.

#### Proof

Expand

\[
n^{-1}\sum_i a_i b_{\pi(i)}
=\bar a\bar b+
n^{-1}\sum_i(a_i-\bar a)(b_{\pi(i)}-\bar b)
\]

and divide by (ar a\bar b).  The second term is the sample correlation
multiplied by the two sample coefficients of variation.  ∎

### Proposition A.2 (finite-sample randomization validity)

If the conditional joint law of the observed labels is invariant under the
declared permutation group and the complete statistic is recomputed on every
orbit element, the randomization p-value is finite-sample valid, up to the
usual Monte Carlo randomization resolution.

This is a group-invariance result and does not follow merely from
(ho_P=0) or (C=1).

### Weak-null studentized permutation boundary

Theorem A.1 and Corollary A.1 establish the sampling limit of the observed
studentized statistic under a regular weak null.  A complete proof that the
conditional permutation distribution converges to the same standard-normal
limit additionally requires a combinatorial triangular-array CLT and
conditional consistency of the fully recomputed orbit studentizer.  The
current simulations support this pointwise candidate, but this appendix does
not claim uniform weak-null permutation validity.  Until that additional
conditional proof is written, the manuscript should distinguish:

1. theorem-level iid Wald asymptotics;
2. exact permutation inference under label invariance; and
3. empirically supported, theoretically motivated studentized permutation
   inference under regular weak nulls.

## A.8 Why the theorem is not uniform near degeneracy

The nuisance expansion can be written schematically as

\[
\widehat\theta-\theta
\approx-J^{-1}(P_n-P)g.
\]

In the least-identified dimensionless direction its size is

\[
O_P\{1/(\sqrt n\,\sigma_{\min}(J))\}.
\]

A1–A7 require nonsingularity at each fixed (P), but they do not bound
(sigma_{\min}(J)) uniformly away from zero over a sequence (P_n).
Therefore Theorem A.1 makes no claim when

\[
\sqrt n\,\sigma_{\min}(J(P_n))=O(1).
\]

In that region, second-order curvature and nonlocal median/MAD selection can
be of the same order as the nominal first-order term.  This is exactly the
boundary examined by the canonical bridge simulations; it is not a
contradiction of the pointwise theorem.
