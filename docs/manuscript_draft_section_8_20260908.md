# Working manuscript: Discussion

Date: 2026-09-08. Section 7 remains an explicit application/scope decision;
this draft does not supply a synthetic substitute for a real application.

## 8. Discussion

### 8.1 Contribution and intended interpretation

This study concerns a specific scientific question: whether matched units have
associated magnitudes of departure from their respective marginal references.
The target \(\rho_P=\operatorname{Corr}(|X-T_X|,|Y-T_Y|)\) does not encode the
directions of those departures. It is neither a test of equality of marginal
distributions nor an omnibus measure of dependence. A scientific application
must justify both the pairing and why deviation magnitude, rather than signed
association or dyadic geometry, is relevant. A robust group reference also
requires justification when a known external standard would answer the
scientific question more directly.

The contribution is not the absolute-value transformation or a broadly new
correlation coefficient. It is the development and examination of inference
for profiles generated by median/MAD-scaled Huber reference fits. The complete
influence expansion identifies where reference estimation enters, while the
failure examples show why robustness of the reference alone is not a
guarantee of stable inference for the generated profiles. The secondary scale
\(C=1+\rho_PCV(a)CV(b)\) retains a historical connection but combines profile
association with marginal radial heterogeneity. It should not replace the
primary estimand merely because it yields a more favorable result.

### 8.2 Pointwise theory is not a finite-sample assurance

Under the stated regular IID assumptions, the complete paired influence
function yields pointwise asymptotic normality and consistent studentization.
This is **pointwise, not uniform** validity. It supplies no minimum safe sample
size, no guarantee near singular reference identification, and no automatic
accuracy under strong skewness. The theorem concerns a well-defined reference
functional and exact or sufficiently accurate empirical roots; implementation
convergence checks and numerical tolerances must be documented separately.

The nonzero-effect correlated-lognormal follow-up makes this limitation
concrete. With active fitted-reference and indirect MAD contributions, nominal
95% complete-IF Wald coverage is .858, .9115, and .927 at sample sizes 160, 640,
and 2560, respectively. All Monte Carlo Wilson intervals exclude .95. The
oracle fixed-reference estimator also undercovers. This cannot be explained
solely by nonregular reference switching: the law in this experiment is fixed
and regular, and a similar deficiency persists when reference estimation is
removed. The full and direct-only IF variances are close in this particular
law because nuisance variance and direct--nuisance covariance nearly cancel.
That cancellation is not a general argument for omitting the reference terms.

A post hoc diagnostic on the same frozen samples separates lower and upper
interval misses. At n=2560, the complete interval falls entirely below the
truth in .0595 of repetitions and entirely above it in .0135, against a
nominal .025 in each tail. Replacing the estimated SE by the infeasible
population asymptotic SE changes total coverage to .958, with corresponding
tail rates .0195 and .0225. At smaller sizes, that diagnostic can be
conservative and does not uniformly restore balanced tails. Error and fitted
SE are positively associated in the stored repetitions. These comparisons
implicate studentization in the observed distortion but do not decompose
causal contributions from bias, skewness, random scale, and their interaction.
The population-variance calculation is an infeasible diagnostic, not a
proposed interval or a data-driven correction.

An exact identity clarifies the mean shift without asserting a higher-order
limit theorem. Let \(\sigma_n=\sqrt{V_\rho/n}\),
\(U_n=(\widehat\rho_P-\rho_P)/\sigma_n\), and
\(\delta_n=\widehat{SE}/\sigma_n-1\). Whenever \(\widehat{SE}>0\),

\[
Z_n=\frac{U_n}{1+\delta_n}
=U_n-U_n\delta_n+\frac{U_n\delta_n^2}{1+\delta_n}.
\]

In the stored n=2560 complete-IF repetitions, the three empirical mean terms
are approximately -.02444, -.10102, and .00435, summing to -.12111. The
interaction term is substantial in this mean decomposition. It is not a
decomposition of coverage error, does not identify a unique causal pathway,
and does not justify assuming the remainder negligible in difficult regimes.

Accordingly, our results do not justify presenting the current Wald procedure
as a generally calibrated finite-sample solution. A paper on inferential
reliability can report this limitation as a substantive finding. A paper that
promises a practically calibrated interval would require additional,
prespecified method development and validation beyond the present claims.

### 8.3 What the switching evidence does and does not establish

The separated-mode construction and paired interventions show that nonlocal
reference fitting **can cause, not always causes** substantial finite-sample
distortion. The severe unbridged signed-lognormal example has a unique median
with zero density at its root; it is not a regular-law application of the
main theorem. Its population Huber location equation is not flat at the
declared scale. The mechanism involves the full fitted median/MAD/Huber
system, including nonlocal movement of empirical medians and MADs and
propagation through the sample scale. At population symmetry, the first-order
MAD coupling vanishes; nonlocal propagation must not be equated with that
first-order derivative.

Fixing references and enforcing exact sign balance are interventions on the
construction, not universally available remedies. The binary imposed-offset
identity explains how aligned reference shifts can create correlation, but
does not determine the probability that a fitted algorithm selects those
shifts. At the exact binary law and zero reference, radial variance is zero
and the target is undefined. A complete switching-probability or
local-to-degeneracy theorem remains distinct from the algebraic identity and
the empirical interventions. We do not claim that theorem here.

### 8.4 What the nuisance index can organize

The index \(I_n=\sqrt n\,\sigma_{\min}(J)\) reflects a local linear
amplification scale for standardized nuisance noise. It is a **first-order
organizer, not a universal cutoff**. Invariance to positive changes of data
units holds under the declared estimating-equation normalization, not under
arbitrary equation rescaling. Moreover, the covariance of the joint nuisance
expansion contains its paired score covariance \(\Omega\), and its impact on
the target depends on the target's nuisance projection. A small singular
value need not have a first-order effect on a target orthogonal to that
direction. The symmetric calibration examples illustrate this distinction.

Grouped and prospective validation supports coarse prediction within the
studied bridge families, while systematic family residuals remain unexplained
by the scalar index. Equal or similar \(J\) does not match the complete
first-order stochastic structure. Curvature, tails, competing fitted-reference
regions, and nonlocal selection are plausible additional contributors, not
an established decomposition of purely higher-order error. The population
index is not currently a sample-level validity certificate. An operational
diagnostic would need estimation-error analysis, a declared action rule, and
prospective assessment of the errors induced by that rule.

### 8.5 Comparators, sampling units, and practical use

The procedure uses a **robust reference, not a globally robust correlation**.
The final unbounded profile products and squared profiles can have high
leverage. Capping changes the estimand; historical cap sensitivity experiments
do not establish an automatically preferable replacement. Pearson, rank
correlation, Mantel methods, and general dependence coefficients answer
different questions and should not be ranked by power as though they shared
one null and one scientific target. Same-target inference comparisons, such
as the direct-only ablation, are more informative about the generated-reference
correction. Even there, better coverage must be considered together with
width, bias, failures, and Monte Carlo uncertainty.

The independent unit is the intact observed pair. Pairwise distances sharing
an observation are not additional IID observations. If buildings are the
independent units, room-level sample size cannot substitute for the number of
buildings; a handful of clusters does not justify an ordinary cluster CLT.
Exact randomization requires the declared group invariance. A weak null of
zero profile correlation alone supplies neither that invariance nor a
conditional studentized permutation CLT. The permutation panels therefore
remain empirical evidence, separately labelled from Wald-theorem calibration.

For a substantive application, report the chosen reference functional and
tuning constants, the pairing and sampling design, profile variability,
fitting failures, and the limitations of the inferential approximation.
Such reporting improves interpretability but is not a substitute for a
validated uncertainty procedure. No real-data analysis or general practical
safe-use rule is claimed in the present draft.

### 8.6 Scope of the present paper and future work

The present evidence supports a bounded methodological account: a defined
profile target, full regular-law inference, an isolated nonlocal fitting
failure, and an informative but incomplete conditioning diagnostic. Numerical
audits, source hashes, and unit tests improve reproducibility; they are not
independent mathematical peer review and do not prove finite-sample validity.

The next scientific decision is whether the paper is primarily an analysis of
reliability and failure, or must additionally deliver a practically calibrated
inferential procedure. If a stronger theoretical bridge is desired, one
carefully chosen local-to-degeneracy result would address an identified gap
more directly than a larger distribution catalogue. Application selection,
clustered or multivariate extensions, a weak-null permutation theorem, and an
operational diagnostic each introduce separate obligations and are not
silently incorporated into the current claims.
