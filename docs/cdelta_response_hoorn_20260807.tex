\begin{quote}
\textbf{Main conclusion.} The robust-centre statistic is best treated as a
separate $\cds$ measuring concordance of observation-level salience profiles.
It is not a robust substitute for every purpose of the original $\cd$.
For a fixed pair of profiles, $\cds$ and Pearson correlation give identical
one-sided permutation evidence. Mantel instead asks whether the complete
dyadic distance patterns agree. The three procedures therefore require
different scientific wording.
\end{quote}

\section{Definitions and the change in research question}

For observations $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and $y_1,\ldots,y_n$, define the original
one-dimensional L2 divergence profiles
\begin{equation}
d_{Xi}=\left\{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)^2\right\}^{1/2},
\qquad
d_{Yi}=\left\{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j\ne i}(y_i-y_j)^2\right\}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The corrected coefficient is
\begin{equation}
\cd=\frac{n^{-1}\sum_i d_{Xi}d_{Yi}}
{(n^{-1}\sum_i d_{Xi})(n^{-1}\sum_i d_{Yi})}.
\end{equation}
It asks whether the same labelled observations have high overall divergence
from their respective groups.

For the new statistic, let $T_X,T_Y$ be Huber locations fitted using fixed MAD
scales $s_X,s_Y$, and set
\begin{equation}
r_{Xi}=\frac{|x_i-T_X|}{s_X},\qquad
r_{Yi}=\frac{|y_i-T_Y|}{s_Y},\qquad
\cds=\frac{n^{-1}\sum_i r_{Xi}r_{Yi}}{\bar r_X\bar r_Y}.
\end{equation}
This asks whether the same observations are salient or atypical in the two
measurements. I therefore agree that the robust-centre version changes the
research question and merits the notation $\cds$. ``Reproducible anomaly'' or
``salience-profile concordance'' is a more accurate description than
``robustified original $\cd$''.

There is still one important qualification. The Huber centre protects the
reference point, but the uncapped radius scores every remote observation at
its full distance. Thus $\cds$ is robust in centre fitting, not bounded in its
final observation leverage.

\section{Question 1 and 2: relationship with Pearson correlation}

For any positive paired profiles $a_i,b_i$ with nonzero variance,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\overline{ab}}{\bar a\bar b}
=1+\operatorname{Cor}(a,b)\operatorname{CV}(a)\operatorname{CV}(b).
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\cds=1+r_{\mathrm{Pearson}}(r_X,r_Y)\,
\operatorname{CV}(r_X)\operatorname{CV}(r_Y).}
\end{equation}

Under permutations of the pairing, the two CVs are fixed and positive.
Therefore $\cds$ and Pearson correlation have exactly the same permutation
ordering, one-sided permutation $p$-value, and rejection decision. They are
not numerically identical estimands: $\cds$ combines concordance with the two
marginal profile CVs, whereas Pearson isolates standardized concordance.

Multiplying either profile by a positive constant cancels from $\cds$ and
leaves Pearson correlation unchanged. Hence using absolute deviations from
the robust centre without MAD normalization gives exactly the same result.

\textbf{Interpretive consequence.} If the inferential question is only
``are the two robust residual profiles positively concordant?'', Pearson plus
the same permutation scheme is sufficient and more familiar. $\cds$ adds a
distinct descriptive scale only if the product of marginal salience CVs is
scientifically meaningful and should amplify or attenuate the concordance.

\section{Original $\cd$ versus Mantel (1967)}

Mantel's procedure compares the corresponding entries of two complete
dissimilarity matrices. In its common standardized form it correlates
\(\{D^X_{ij}:i<j\}\) with \(\{D^Y_{ij}:i<j\}\), and obtains a reference
distribution by simultaneously permuting rows and columns (unit labels) of
one matrix \cite{mantel1967,shi2024}.

The original $\cd$ first compresses each distance matrix to one row summary
per observation. In one-dimensional L2 form there is an exact identity
\begin{equation}
d_{Xi}^2=\frac{n}{n-1}\left\{(x_i-\bar x)^2+s_{X,\mathrm{pop}}^2\right\}.
\label{eq:l2row}
\end{equation}
Thus the entire row profile is a monotone function of absolute centred
magnitude. It does not remember which other observations are above or below
$x_i$, or which dyads generated the row total.

To demonstrate the distinction, 40 observations were arranged as 20
opposite-sign pairs with fixed absolute radii. Signs were independently
rewired between $X$ and $Y$ while preserving each observation's absolute
centred radius. Equation~\eqref{eq:l2row} then makes the two L2 profiles
identical for every replication.

Therefore Mantel does not make $\cd$ redundant, but $\cd$ cannot be described
as preserving all pairwise differences. A precise statement is:

\begin{quote}
$\cd$ compares which labelled units have similar levels of overall divergence
within their groups. Mantel compares whether the complete pattern of labelled
pairwise distances agrees.
\end{quote}

\subsection{Across buildings}

The claim that $\cd$ compares across buildings is defensible only if units
have meaningful correspondence and the scientific target is node-level global
divergence. It should not be stated as comparing the complete disease-versus-
ventilation geometry across apartments.

Building heterogeneity must also be reflected in the permutation design. In
12 high-replication conditional-null conditions (1,500 datasets each), pooling
and permuting across blocks produced severe false rejection, whereas
within-building permutations restored calibration:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Mean null rejection across heterogeneous-block conditions.}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Method & Unrestricted permutation & Within-building permutation\\
\midrule
Original L2 $\cd$ & 0.4417 & 0.0481\\
Huber $\cds$ & 0.5158 & 0.0474\\
Cap-6 sensitivity & 0.5201 & 0.0473\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two separately calculated Mantel correlations should not simply be compared
by an ordinary Fisher-$z$ difference because dyadic entries are dependent.
If the aim is to compare relationship strength across buildings, a
unit/block-level permutation or bootstrap interaction test is preferable. If
the aim is to explain a disease-distance matrix by ventilation distance plus
building and other distance predictors, MRQAP is the closer framework
\cite{dekker2007}. Mantel and partial Mantel also require caution under spatial
autocorrelation \cite{guillot2013}.

\section{Status of meaningful outliers}

I agree with the proposed distinction:
\begin{description}
\item[RQ1: exact group structure.] If the exceptional member genuinely
belongs to this particular team, its influence is part of the phenomenon.
The original $\cd$ is a legitimate descriptive statistic, and strong L2
weighting may be intentional.
\item[RQ2: stable or transferable structure.] If the target is the ordinary
or reproducible pattern beyond this exact group, leverage control and
sensitivity analyses become necessary.
\end{description}

However, ``the outlier belongs'' and ``its squared leverage is optimal'' are
different propositions. Inclusion is a scientific decision; the exponent or
cap is an estimand-weighting decision.

The following experiment used 1,000 independent $n=60$ datasets per condition.
One high observation was inserted at the same index (matched) or different
indices (unmatched). ``Top share'' is the largest single contribution to the
numerator divided by the entire numerator.

The original L2 outlier changes ordinary members' divergence scores because
every row contains a distance to it. At magnitude 32, the mean relative shift
among ordinary L2 profile entries was about 2.28. For Huber radii it was only
about 0.046: the centre is protected and spillover is small. Yet the
uncapped matched observation itself then dominates the product numerator.
This separates two kinds of robustness:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{reference robustness}: ordinary observations are not moved much;
\item \textbf{final leverage robustness}: no single observation dominates the
coefficient.
\end{enumerate}
Uncapped $\cds$ has the first property but not the second. Cap 6 is a separate,
bounded sensitivity estimand that provides the second property.

\section{Recent inferential findings}

For the uncapped Huber $\cds$, a complete functional-delta influence function
has been derived. It includes the median and MAD effects on both Huber centres,
the numerator and two denominator moments, and all covariance induced by the
paired $(X_i,Y_i)$ observations. Under iid pairs, continuous positive marginal
densities at the median/MAD points, unique positive MADs and Huber roots, no
mass at derivative knots, positive radius means, and finite joint squared
radius-product moments,
\begin{equation}
\sqrt n(\widehat{\cds}-\cds)
=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n IF_{\cds}(X_i,Y_i)+o_P(1)
\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,V).
\end{equation}

Population-level skew-lognormal quadrature matched regular-point
contamination derivatives to scaled error below $1.2\times10^{-5}$. The
theory itself is supported. Finite-sample studentization is not yet reliable:
true analytic densities and cross-fitted KDE changed mean SE by at most 0.2\%;
HC-style correction remained below nominal coverage; and complete-refit
bootstrap-$t$ did not improve coverage. Therefore permutation inference
remains the formal default, with the sandwich interval labelled exploratory.

\section{Recommended terminology and reporting}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Keep \textbf{original $\cd$}: exact-set L2 divergence-profile
concordance. State explicitly that it summarizes row-level/global divergence,
not full dyadic geometry.
\item Introduce \textbf{$\cds$}: Huber-reference absolute-salience-profile
concordance. Avoid claiming bounded robustness for the uncapped version.
\item Report \textbf{Pearson robust-profile correlation} alongside $\cds$, or
use Pearson as the inferential statistic, because the permutation test is
identical. Retain $\cds$ only when its $CV_XCV_Y$ weighting is substantively
desired.
\item Keep \textbf{cap-6 $\cds$} as a pre-specified sensitivity estimand, not a
post-hoc correction and not the same research question as the original $\cd$.
\item Use \textbf{Mantel/QAP or MRQAP} when the hypothesis concerns complete
dyadic distance correspondence or multiple distance-matrix predictors.
\item For multiple buildings, define whether the target is pooled,
within-building, or an interaction before choosing the permutation scheme.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Immediate next checks}

\begin{itemize}
\item Decide whether the $CV_XCV_Y$ weighting in Equation~\eqref{eq:identity}
is part of the intended construct. This is now the most important definition
decision for $\cds$.
\item Test Mantel, profile Pearson, original $\cd$, and $\cds$ on one shared
building-style data generator with dyadic versus node-salience alternatives.
\item Formalize an across-building interaction/permutation design using
matched rooms or explicitly unmatched units.
\item Derive the cap-6 influence function separately if bounded final leverage
is to be claimed formally.
\end{itemize}
